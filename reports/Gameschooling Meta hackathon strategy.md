# Compile the curriculum, verify the kid, win the Indie track

**BLUF.** The highest-probability path to first place in the Indie Developer track is to stop describing GameSchooling as a "gamified learning platform" and ship it as a *parent-authored curriculum compiler with verifiable grading*: the parent writes the curriculum, worldview constraints and house rules; a Muse Wonder orchestrator and Muse Spark 1.3 sub-agents compile them into typed quests, exams and rubrics; children prove learning through quizzes, real-world photo missions segmented by SAM 3.1, and voice exams transcribed by Muse Voice Transcribe; Muse Image draws a consistent quest world; and no point is paid into the family ledger until an Examiner verdict is corroborated by the open-weight Muse Spark Open on Baseten and logged in a parent-visible Agent Console. That shape mirrors the last three Cerebral Valley gold medals (a lawyer, a doctor and a teacher building the tool they personally needed), uses six Meta models in roles the docs themselves describe (Wonder plans and delegates, Spark runs tight tool loops, SAM "segments every match for a noun phrase"), and explicitly avoids the two anti-projects most likely to kill an education entry ("simple 'AI for Education' chatbots" and "Basic RAG"). The market case is real but must be framed as a family-learning OS rather than a homeschool niche: 3.4M US homeschoolers growing 5.4%/yr, 126k in England growing 13%/yr, and 35% of US parents already paying for supplemental learning, with no incumbent at the intersection of parent-authored content, worldview-agnostic curricula, multi-child economy and multimodal evidence. The pedagogy evidence forces three design changes to the original concept: points reward mastery not compliance, "risk" is an opt-in stake rather than a deduction, and the sibling leaderboard ships opt-in and relative beneath a family co-op bar. Roughly 36 hours remain; the plan below allots ~21 build hours, ~5.5 hours for video and submission, ~7 hours of sleep and a buffer, with a feature freeze at Nov 4 20:00 GMT+7 and a complete submission by ~01:00 Nov 5, eight hours before the deadline.

## No incumbent sells parent-authored, worldview-agnostic family learning

The core segment is large and growing at a pace that makes the Impact criterion credible. NHERI estimates **3.408 million** US K-12 homeschool students in 2024-25 (range ~3.07–3.75M), **6.262%** of the school-age population, up from 3.135M in 2021-22; NHERI is an advocacy research institute, so treat it as an estimate ([NHERI](https://nheri.org/?p=309582) [K]). Johns Hopkins' Homeschool Hub measured **5.4% average growth** in 2024-25 across 22 states with data, nearly three times the ~2% pre-pandemic rate, with over a third of reporting states at all-time highs and growth up to 21.5% in South Carolina ([Reason](https://reason.com/2025/11/19/homeschooling-hits-record-numbers); [JHU edpolicy](https://education.jhu.edu/edpolicy/?p=1793) [K]). England's Department for Education counted **126,000** children in elective home education on the autumn 2025 census day, up 13% in a year, with **175,900** home-educated at some point in 2024/25 ([DfE](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/elective-home-education/2025-26-autumn-term) [K]). A Dec 2025 survey of 1,424 US homeschool parents found 86% plan to continue and named limited social interaction, time management, and "keeping students focused and motivated" as the top challenges, which is precisely the problem a gamified, family-co-op product addresses ([FLVS](https://flvs.net/about-us/who-we-are/newsroom/news-releases/2025/12/02/new-survey-finds-86--of-u.s.-families-plan-to-continue-homeschooling) [K]).

The adjacent segment is far larger and should lead the messaging: **35%** of US parents have a child in supplemental learning programs ([Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/over-one-third-of-parents-have-enrolled-their-students-in-supplemental-22558425) [K]), US parents state willingness to pay an average **$357/month** for tutoring ([EdChoice](https://www.edchoice.org/2025-a-kaleidoscope-view-of-k12-tutoring-in-america/) [K]), UK private tuition runs ~GBP 2,758/child/yr for a ~GBP 6B market ([DJS Research](https://www.djsresearch.co.uk/EducationMarketResearchInsightsAndFindings/article/Education-Sector-Market-Research-Shows-Private-Tuition-Booming-in-UK-01182) [K]), and the education-apps market is ~USD 6.01B (2024) heading to ~7.27B (2025 est.) ([Straits Research](https://straitsresearch.com/report/education-apps-market) [K]). For the user's own region the quantitative case is weak and should not be used in the submission: Indonesia has no official count (a ">500,000" claim on a tuition site is **UNVERIFIED**), Malaysia estimates run 3,000–5,000 families, but the qualitative case (Islamic-values motives, PKBM/Paket equivalency pathway, Cambridge/IGCSE homeschool demand, and safety-driven interest after a 2025 school stabbing in Malaysia) is strong and underserved by English-first US products ([Atlantis Press](https://www.atlantis-press.com/proceedings/icei-24/126009679); [The Sun Malaysia](https://thesun.my/news/malaysia-news/safety-concerns-push-parents-towards-homeschooling) [K]). One naming caution: "gameschooling" is a term culturally owned by the board-game homeschool community (Cait Curley's My Little Poppies; a 2025/26 *Gameschooling For Dummies*), so a digital product should bridge to real-world play, which the photo-mission mechanic does ([Art of Problem Solving](https://blog.artofproblemsolving.com/articles/your-guide-to-gameschooling-for-all-ages-with-cait-curley) [K]).

The competitive map shows every incumbent owning exactly one axis. Prodigy (>100M registered users, memberships $9.95–29.95/month *per child*) and Synthesis Tutor ($29/month family plan, up to 7 kids) own engagement with fixed content; Time4Learning ($39.95/month per student, raised from ~$33 in April 2026) and Miacademy (~$42/month) own curriculum coverage; Monarch, BJU, Abeka and Classical Conversations (140,000+ students, $500–1,500/yr) each own a single Christian worldview; Islamic kids apps (Quran Era $9.99/month, Ali Huda $7.99/month) own a single Islamic one; Joon ($12.99/month) and Greenlight ($4.99–5.99/month for up to 5 kids) own the home chore-and-allowance economy; and the 2025-26 AI-native cohort (DoodlePad, Edusina, Anthos, HomeLearnAI) generates plans for the parent with no child-facing game loop ([Prodigy blog](https://prodigygame.com/blog/ways-to-save-on-prodigy-memberships); [synthesis.com/tutor](https://www.synthesis.com/tutor); [time4learning.com](https://time4learning.com/product/pricing.html); [Carolina Journal](https://www.carolinajournal.com/as-homeschooling-blossoms-n-c-grown-curriculum-stands-out/); [Quran Era](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1582134218); [NeuroLaunch](https://neurolaunch.com/joon-app-review/); [Greenlight](https://greenlight.com/gohenry-vs-greenlight); [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/doodlepad) [K]). Nobody sells "many worldviews, one engine", parent-authored game mechanics, family tenancy at one price for n children, or multimodal evidence that lets pre-readers participate. The reference app "Ayyubi" (the Study screen at chabibachmad-dev.github.io, which the market note describes as the founder's reference app) already proves parents will use AI-generated MCQ lessons, parent-defined rewards and penalties, daily caps, and a leaderboard, in Indonesian, on a PIN-based single device; what it lacks, verified by absence in its string table, is a curriculum/competency model, any pedagogical or worldview layer, a toddler mode, real-world missions, photo input, multi-parent roles and family tenancy ([Ayyubi bundle](https://chabibachmad-dev.github.io/ringkasan_harian/assets/index-BCwdAOL1.js) [K]). That is the baseline GameSchooling must visibly exceed, and (**DECISION**, see rules section) none of its code should be imported.

Complaints in the category tell you what not to ship. Fairplay's FTC complaint against Prodigy centres on pay-to-win social comparison: members "advance more quickly, are given extra strength in battles, and earn more points" and "children see each other's scores" ([Fairplay](https://fairplayforkids.org/prodigy-losing-equation/) [K]). Joon reviewers call $12.99/month "too expensive for struggling families" and report kids losing interest within weeks ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/IN/app/id1482225056) [K]). Price anchoring therefore favours a family plan at ~$9–15/month (or ~$99/yr) for unlimited children, one-third of a single Time4Learning seat, with purchasing-power pricing (e.g. IDR 49–99k/month) for Indonesia and Malaysia, and an ESA/education-savings-account channel in US states (Goally is ESA-reimbursable) ([Goally support](https://goally.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/69000865169-i-m-an-esa-parent-how-do-i-renew-my-goally-subscription-out-of-pocket-) [K]). Unit economics will require per-day generation caps, as the reference app already does.

## Pedagogy evidence forces three changes to the original economy

Gamification works on average but modestly, and the mechanisms that carry the effect are the ones GameSchooling should build around. Huang et al. (2020) pooled 30 studies (3,083 participants) at **g = .464** favouring gamification; Sailer & Homner (2020) found small significant effects and identified *game fiction* and *social interaction* as the significant moderators; Bai, Hew & Huang (2020) found learners enjoyed gamification for feedback and recognition and disliked it when it added no utility or caused anxiety or jealousy ([ETR&D](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11423-020-09807-z.pdf); [Augsburg repository](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/frontdoor/index/index/docId/109056); [ECNU](https://pure.ecnu.edu.cn/en/publications/the-impact-of-gamification-in-educational-settings-on-student-lea/) [P]). Self-Determination Theory supplies the design primitives: in-game autonomy, competence and relatedness independently predict enjoyment and continued play ([Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006](https://www.rochester.edu/warner/lida/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/02bfe513dd59366750000000.pdf) [P]). Learning science supplies the scheduling: spaced retrieval beats massed retrieval at **g = 0.74** across 29 studies, and mastery learning averages **0.52 SD** across 108 controlled evaluations ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10648-021-09595-9); [Kulik et al. 1990](https://www.academia.edu/81783373/Effectiveness_of_Mastery_Learning_Programs_A_Meta_Analysis) [P]).

The risk is equally well documented. Deci, Koestner & Ryan's meta-analysis of **128 experiments** found tangible rewards substantially undermine intrinsic motivation, and the effect is "more detrimental for children than college students"; the original Lepper, Greene & Nisbett study showed preschoolers who liked drawing drew less after being promised a reward ([UW PDF](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf); [Ewha summary](https://pure.ewha.ac.kr/en/publications/a-meta-analytic-review-of-experiments-examining-the-effects-of-ex/); [Structural Learning](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/overjustification-effect) [P][K]). Leaderboard studies show students prefer *relative* boards (neighbours only) and that *absolute* boards raise peer pressure on middle- and bottom-ranked students; competitive framing lowered intrinsic motivation in 5th–6th graders and 7- and 9-year-old girls benefited most from solo play ([HKU 2025](https://hub.hku.hk/bitstream/10722/368255/1/content.pdf); [Murdoch](https://researchportal.murdoch.edu.au/esploro/outputs/conferencePaper/The-myth-of-cooperative-learning-and/991005542704007891); [Zaphiris 2007](https://ktisis.cut.ac.cy/bitstream/20.500.14279/1781/6/Zaphiris2007-Individualistic_versus_competitive_game-based_e-learning.pdf) [P]). Inside families the stakes are higher: parental differential treatment is linked to riskier youth behaviour and poorer sibling relationships, especially for the less-favoured child, and KU researchers advise parents to "treat your children equally or explain why not" ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5685545); [KU News](https://news.ku.edu/news/article/2023/07/25/parents-treat-your-children-equally-or-explain-why-not) [P]). Public point deductions are the specific ClassDojo critique, and the UK ICO's draft Children's Code barred reward loops that exploit reward-seeking (whether that wording survived to the final code is **UNVERIFIED**, ICO pages returned 403) ([EducationHQ](https://educationhq.com/news/classdojo-harms-teachers-behaviour-management-approach-researcher-188825/); [SCL](https://www.scl.org/10795-ico-publishes-final-version-of-age-appropriate-design-code/) [P]).

The reconciliation with the user's "risk/reward economy and sibling leaderboard" is therefore a set of rules, not a rejection, and they double as the product's "craft" story. Points are evidence of effort or mastery, never of login or time; they are delivered immediately, small, and paired with a parent-facing specific-praise prompt, with a published "price inflation" schedule as the fading mechanism the token-economy literature recommends ([Vanderbilt tip sheet](https://vkc.vumc.org/assets/files/triad/ffp/Challenging_Behaviors/Token_Economy_Tip_Sheet.pdf) [P]). **"Risk" becomes an opt-in stake**: the child wagers a bonus multiplier on a harder quest and can lose only the bonus, never the base balance. Parent-defined penalties map to privileges the parent already controls, require a stated reason, are logged, are never automatic and are never shown to siblings (**DECISION**: the `penalty_rules.auto` flag in the schema should ship hard-wired to false, and the Quest Master's tool should be renamed from `apply_penalty` to `propose_consequence`, which only creates a pending item for parent approval). Streaks, if present, are weekly for under-10s with automatic (never purchased) freezes, borrowing Duolingo's finding that two equipped Streak Freezes raised DAU +0.38% and that 7-day streakers are 3.6× likelier to finish ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/) [P]). The default social surface is a **family co-op bar** (one shared weekly goal with a shared real-world reward), a personal-best board is on by default, and the **sibling leaderboard is opt-in per child, relative (neighbours only), and age-normalised** (effort minutes, mastery deltas) rather than raw points. Whenever a parent sets different prices or thresholds per child, the system asks for a one-line reason that lands in the transparency log. A weekly 10-minute family review, where the child narrates what they learned (Charlotte Mason's narration doubling as intrinsic support) and points convert to rewards by a human hand, closes the loop.

Age bands drive modality, and they are what makes the toddler tier possible with near-zero child screen time. WHO advises no screens under 1 and no more than one hour a day at ages 2–4, and the AAP recommends a per-child Family Media Plan ([NBC NY on WHO](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/health/Screen-Time-Guidelines-Babies-Kids-Under-Five-508999771.html); [AAP](https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/media-and-children/) [P]). The attention heuristic of ~2–3 minutes per year of age is a parenting-site rule of thumb with no primary study located (**UNVERIFIED as science, usable as a default**) ([Self Sufficient Kids](https://selfsufficientkids.com/average-attention-span-by-age-for-children/) [P]).

Grading must be designed for the known failure modes of LLM graders. Henkel, Vanacore & Roberts (2025) found LLMs match human reliability on story-retell grading with a well-designed rubric but struggle on ambiguous responses and recommend hybrid systems with humans handling nuance; Deng et al. (2025) found strong agreement for binary grading that weakens as rubric granularity increases, and that filtering low-confidence predictions raises accuracy on the remainder ([ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.aimecon-sessions.8); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08843) [P]). The assessment contract that follows: every generated item carries `answer_key` or a 2–4-level `rubric`, a `grading_mode` in {exact, numeric_tolerance, mcq, rubric, parent_verify}, `expected_evidence` and `max_points`; the pipeline tries a deterministic check first, then a rubric-conditioned Spark grade returning `score`, `confidence` and an `evidence_quote`, then a second opinion, and anything below threshold or in disagreement becomes `needs_parent` with provisional points and a one-tap parent override that is logged. For photos, the grader first *transcribes* what it sees so the parent can confirm the reading cheaply; for recitation (Quran, memory verses, poems) the model grades word accuracy only and routes pronunciation and tajwid to the parent, because the model cannot and should not claim it. The mastery gate is ~80% on a mixed retrieval set, re-tested after a spacing interval (the 80% figure is practitioner convention, not from the meta-analysis, **flagged in [P]**).

Every pedagogical approach a parent might pick reduces to one six-field profile that the LLM is prompted with: philosophy, subjects/strands, progression unit, assessment style, lesson grammar, and worldview constraints as explicit allow/deny lists so one model can serve a secular-Montessori and an Islamic-classical family without cross-contamination ([P] KQ1). Only a few sources have machine-readable objectives: Cambridge Primary Maths 0096 uses codes like `2Ni.01` (stage + strand + number) ([Cambridge 0096 PDF mirror](https://josefs-international.squarespace.com/s/Cambridge-Primary-Mathematics-Curriculum-Framework-0096_tcm142-592530.pdf) [P]), Common Core math exists as JSONL on Hugging Face ([allenai/achieve-the-core](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/achieve-the-core/blob/06a0a3980e618f21ade4ab31a11a6d0579af6a52/README.md) [P]), CEFR young-learner descriptors are validated for ages 7–10 and 11–15 ([Council of Europe](https://rm.coe.int/the-collation-of-validated-portfolio-descriptors-for-young-learners-go/1680788b28) [P]), and Kurikulum Merdeka writes outcomes per *fase* (A = SD 1–2 … F = SMA 11–12) ([Quipper](https://www.quipper.com/id/blog/info-guru/fase-kurikulum-merdeka/) [P]). The **seed-now set for 36 hours** is six profiles that cover the widest mechanics with the least hand-encoding: Singapore Math P1–4 (CPA sequence, photo-of-manipulatives → bar model), Cambridge Primary Maths/Science (coded objectives, exam-style grading), Common Core ELA+Math K–5 (JSONL available), Charlotte Mason Years 1–3 via AmblesideOnline (voice narration, nature-photo missions), Classical Grammar stage (recitation), and an Islamic track (Juz 30 hifz + Safar-style studies L1–3, with parent override for recitation); Kurikulum Merdeka Fase A/B and a life-skills/financial-literacy track are optional seventh and eighth ([P] KQ8). Each needs the profile JSON, 10–20 competency nodes, 3 lesson templates, the assessment contract and one deny-list. The stack is chosen for zero setup friction: Next.js 16.4 (App Router, note `middleware.ts` is now `proxy.ts`), Tailwind + shadcn/ui, Supabase (Postgres + Auth + Realtime + Storage + RLS), Vercel (Hobby: 300 s function max, **4.5 MB request body cap**, so photos and audio upload from the browser straight to Supabase Storage), Vercel AI SDK 7 with `@ai-sdk/openai-compatible` pointed at `https://api.meta.ai/v1` for streaming and structured output, the raw `openai` npm SDK for Images/Responses/SAM, and plain `fetch` for ASR ([AI SDK OpenAI-compatible providers](https://ai-sdk.dev/providers/openai-compatible-providers); [Supabase SSR Next.js](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/server-side/nextjs); [Vercel Functions limits](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limits) [B]). ## Rubric arithmetic: be exceptional on Impact and Demo, prove craft through verifiability

Stage Two scores each criterion 0–10 and weights them, so a project that is merely fine everywhere loses to one that is exceptional on Impact and Demo, which together carry 55%. Impact is won in the first ten seconds of the video and the first two sentences of the description by stating the identity and the mechanism: "I homeschool my kids; I write the curriculum; Meta's models compile it into a game and verify what they learned." That line must be literally true for the user (**flag: the notes assume the user homeschools; confirm before recording**), because judges in this format have rewarded the lived-problem archetype three times running ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/meet-the-winners-of-our-built-with-opus-4-6-claude-code-hackathon) [S]). Impact also asks "Could this actually become something people use?", which the market numbers above answer, and "Does it fit into one of the problem statements?", which the Build for Your World wording ("Solve a problem you know firsthand, free up time and mental space") answers almost word for word if the parent dashboard shows the week's lesson prep collapsing from hours to one compile ([E]).

Demo is won by the rule every guide converges on: a strong project with a confusing demo loses to a simpler project the judges understand ([JetBrains blog](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/), snippet only; [Devpost](https://info.devpost.com/blog/6-tips-for-making-a-hackathon-demo-video) [S]). Judges "typically have about five minutes per project" and will not clone and run the code, so the live product must be a public URL with a seeded family, printed credentials and a five-step judge path, with the video recorded from that URL so the address bar proves deployment ([dev.to (Jacklyn Biggin)](https://dev.to/jacklyn/how-to-make-your-devpost-submissions-not-suck-37i0); [Supabase hackathon docs](https://hackathon.supabase.com/docs) [S]). Every model response on the demo path should be cached by input hash so the path runs even if the API fails, with a visible "Regenerate with live Meta models" button so judges can watch a real call; the README should say so, because caching the demo path is craft, not cheating, as long as live calls remain possible ([HackerEarth](https://www.hackerearth.com/blog/10-tips-win-hackathon) [S]). A participant in this very event runs a Vercel URL with a `pnpm walkthrough` journey runner and 128/128 validation assertions documented in the README, which sets the bar for "holds up live" ([meta-ai-hackathon/meta-hack](https://github.com/meta-ai-hackathon/meta-hack) [S]).

Use of Meta AI Tooling is won by combination and vocabulary. The docs' own phrases to reuse verbatim in the architecture card, README and form are "sub-agent delegation", "parallel tool calls", "structured output", "segments every match for a noun phrase", "speaker diarization", and "anchored generation" ([Meta Model API docs](https://dev.meta.ai/docs/models.md) [S][M]). The single most defensible "surprised even us" claim is SAM 3.1 noun-phrase masks as a *grading primitive* for children's real-world missions, followed by the open-weight model auditing the hosted model before any point is paid, and diarization distinguishing parent prompt from child answer in a voice exam; none of these combinations appears in Meta's cookbook, whose closest recipes are "perception grounding", the "Muse robotics" demo orchestrating Voice + Spark + SAM + Image, and "headlocked captions" mapping diarized voices to tracked people ([Cookbook index](https://dev.meta.ai/docs/cookbook); [cookbook repo](https://github.com/meta-models/meta-model-cookbook) [M][B]). Including the open-weight path also satisfies both halves of the Stage One gate ("Meta Model API and/or Meta open-weight models").

Technical Depth is won cheaply with three visible artifacts: typed JSON schemas for Lesson, Quest, Rubric and Grade in `lib/schemas/`, a verifier that rejects malformed output, and the Agent Console page over `agent_runs` showing model, tokens, cost, latency and evidence thumbnails per call. Those map to the verifiability patterns Meta publishes in its Muse Code cookbook section: append-only audit log, deterministic replay, staged approvals that fail closed, and goal tracking where "work is not closed until acceptance checks pass" ([Cookbook index](https://dev.meta.ai/docs/cookbook) [M]). Commit cadence is evidence too: Elisa's write-up cited "76 commits" in 30 hours, and guides note judges sometimes check commit history to confirm the work happened in the window, so the plan calls for 20+ small conventional commits pushed to `main` and never squashed ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/meet-the-winners-of-our-built-with-opus-4-6-claude-code-hackathon); [dev.to (Arindam)](https://dev.to/arindam_1729/how-to-use-github-effectively-at-hackathons-4emh) [S]).

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of GameSchooling more than it changes the feature list. The product the user described (family servers, composable curricula, parent-authored economy, sibling leaderboard, agents everywhere) is sound, but the version that wins this rubric is the one that can be stated as a compiler and a verifier rather than as a platform: the parent is the author, Wonder plans, Spark executes, SAM and Voice Transcribe turn the physical world and a child's voice into gradable evidence, and an open-weight model audits the hosted one before a single point moves. That sentence simultaneously maximises Impact (a lived problem with a clear path to use), Use of Meta Tooling (six models in roles Meta's own docs describe, plus two combinations the cookbook does not), Technical Depth (typed schemas, a ledger, an audit console) and Demo (a real syllabus, a real backyard, a real voice), and it is structurally immune to the two anti-project readings that would otherwise be the first thing a triage judge thinks of when they see "education".

The second shift is that the pedagogy evidence is not a constraint on the concept but its strongest differentiator. Every competitor either hard-codes mechanics and sells perks (Prodigy, which drew an FTC complaint for exactly that) or has no child loop at all; a product whose economy is parent-authored, mastery-contingent, opt-in on risk, co-op by default and transparent about differential treatment is both the ethically defensible design and the one nobody else sells. Build the opt-in stake, the family co-op bar and the consequence-approval queue as first-class features and say so in the video, and the "Skinner box" critique becomes a talking point in the product's favour. Everything else is execution discipline: smoke-test every endpoint in hour one, freeze at 20:00 on Nov 4, record while alert, and submit eight hours early.
