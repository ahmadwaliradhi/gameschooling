## Rules (verbatim)
- Open source: Repositories must be public.
- Team size: max 4. Solo allowed.
- Demo requirements: "Your demo must only highlight the specific features, code, and functionality that you built during the Hackathon. Judges must be able to clearly identify what was created during the Hackathon. Failure to clearly identify your original contributions will result in immediate disqualification at Judges sole discretion."
- New work only: "What you present at this Hackathon must have been built during the event. This should be clear in your submissions. Startups may build on existing products or codebases, but you must clearly identify what was newly developed during the Hackathon."
- Banned: violate legal/ethical/platform policies; use code, data, or assets you do not have rights to.
- Sample Anti-Projects (generic or policy-violating; avoid): "Any project using AI to generate and give medical advice e.g. AI Mental Health Advisor, skin blemish detector, etc.; Basic RAG Applications; Simple Image Analyzers; simple 'AI for Education' Chatbots; AI Job Application Screener; AI Nutrition Coach; Personality Analyzers; Sports analyzers or coaches; Anything political or involving war"
- Waiver: submission must be original work; open source allowed if licenses followed and submission enhances/builds on it. Participants grant organizers perpetual license to use submission for marketing. Entrant retains IP ownership. Must be 18+. Sanctioned countries excluded.

## Judging (verbatim)
- Stage One: pass/fail — project reasonably fits theme and reasonably used Meta Model API and/or Meta open-weight models to build the project in the Hackathon.
- Stage Two: 0-10 scale per criterion:
  - Impact (30%): "What's the real-world potential here? Who benefits, and how much does it matter? Could this actually become something people use? Does it fit into one of the problem statements listed above?"
  - Use of Meta AI Tooling (25%): "How creatively did this team use Meta's AI tooling? Did they go beyond a basic integration? Did they surface capabilities that surprised even us?"
  - Technical Depth & Execution (20%): "Is the engineering sound and thoughtfully refined? Does this feel like something that was thought through with real craft."
  - Demo (25%): "Is this a working, impressive demo? Does the product hold up live? Is the demo video genuinely cool to watch?"
- Stage Three: top 5 per track; final judges watch video demos live and rank with same rubric.
- Track descriptions: Indie developer track "For individual builders and students. Judged on creativity, technical ambition, and whether it works." Startup track "Judged on technical execution and whether it holds up in production."
- Primary artifacts judges use: 3-minute demo video, code, live product.
